Why is a stack the right choice for undo/redo?
    Stacking uses the last in, first out principle when a coder is writing code. 
    The visual aid that was given was the idea of stacking plates on top of one another, 
    the top plate is always chosen first before the one under it. This data structure is 
    the best option for tasks that may need back tracking or the need to be reversed.

Why is a queue better suited for the help desk?
    Queues follow a similar yet different structure that stacking has, however the major
    difference between the two is that queues use a principle of first in, first out instead
    of the stacking principle which again is last in, first out. This structure is better 
    suited for the help desk because in this situation the person that was there first is 
    given assistance before the person that came after them.
    
How do your implementations differ from Python’s built-in lists?
    The implementations that I used involved the use of nodes and pointers to manage data in stacks
    and queues. Python's built-in lists store collections of data that you’re able to add/remove
    from using .pop() or .append(). Within my implementations I was able to better understand how
    stacks and queues function behind the scenes and operate when tested whereas Python allows for
    an easier experience because the lists are already created for you to use.
